General Questions

A clinical trial is a research study conducted with human volunteers to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of new treatments, interventions, or medical devices. These trials are essential for advancing medical knowledge and improving healthcare for everyone.

By participating, you can play a vital role in medical research, gain access to innovative treatments before they are widely available, and receive expert medical care. Many people also find satisfaction in contributing to scientific advancements that could help others in the future.

Yes, our platform is completely free for participants. Our mission is to connect you with clinical trials that match your needs without any cost to you.

Participation & Eligibility

You can use our search bar to look for trials based on a medical condition, location, or other keywords. Each trial listing includes detailed eligibility criteria, so you can see if you might be a suitable candidate before you apply.

Each study has specific requirements to ensure participant safety and the quality of the research data. If you don't qualify for one trial, don't be discouraged. New trials are added to our platform regularly, and you can set up alerts to be notified of new opportunities that match your profile.

Yes, participation in any clinical trial is completely voluntary. You have the right to withdraw from a study at any time, for any reason.

Compensation & Safety

Many clinical trials offer compensation for your time and travel expenses. The amount and payment schedule vary from study to study. All compensation details are clearly outlined in the trial information provided before you agree to participate.

All clinical trials have potential risks, which can range from minor side effects to more serious complications. Before you enroll, the research team is required to provide you with an "informed consent" document that fully explains all known potential risks and benefits, so you can make an informed decision.

Absolutely. All clinical trials are governed by strict privacy regulations (like HIPAA) to protect your personal health information. Your data is anonymized and kept confidential.
